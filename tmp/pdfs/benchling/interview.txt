          Preparing    for  your   Software    Engineer     Interview               
                                                                                    
          at Benchling                                                              
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          We are excited to (virtually) meet you! We hope it’ll be a great experience where you can meet future
          teammates, learn more about the company, and show us why you would be a great addition to our
          team.                                                                     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          This guide will give you insight into what to expect and how to prepare. We know interviewing can be
          nerve-wracking, so we aim to be transparent about the process - it’s a quick overview of what to
          expect at Benchling.                                                      
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Interview Process                                                         
                                                                                    
          Stage 1: Remote Technical Interview                                       
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          The interview process starts with an hour long technical Zoom with an engineer. In this interview, we'll
          work on a coding problem— in a shared coding environment (Coderpad) that exercises some of the
          skills we value for this role. You should expect to brainstorm and code up a working solution. You
          should expect to write readable, maintainable code and use data structures that you would use
          everyday.                                                                 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Stage 2: Remote “Onsite” Interviews                                       
                                                                                    
          The “onsite” will usually take 5 hours over Zoom.                         
                                                                                    
          We typically split the onsite over two days to help you stay sharp and avoid Zoom fatigue. It also
          allows us to get an early read on the process. If concerns arise that make an offer unlikely, we can
          end the interviews early, saving time for both sides. However, we can adjust to your
          preference—whether you'd like to do it all in one day or spread it over more than two. Just let your
                                                                                    
          recruiter know!

          During the onsite, we assess candidates on problem-solving, architecture, programming productivity,
          and communication and collaboration. Here's a sample onsite schedule:     
                                                                                    
            ●  Product Demo - 20 minutes                                            
               We start the onsite with a product demo, to give you a sense of what we’re building. This is
                                                                                    
               not an assessment or anything you need to prepare for!               
                                                                                    
            ●  Programming Productivity and Problem Solving Interview (coding) - 60 minutes
               This session will be a collaborative coding exercise that includes debugging, problem
               solving, and coding an extension.                                    
                                                                                    
            ●  Data Modeling & Problem Solving Interview (whiteboarding) - 60 minutes
                                                                                    
               You’ll spend this session designing a system and data model to support a set of
               requirements. You’ll work collaboratively with an engineer.          
                                                                                    
            ●  Code Architecture & Programming Productivity Interview (coding) - 90 minutes
                                                                                    
               This session focuses on code design and building an extensible architecture. Rather than
               simply solving a single problem, you’ll define the building blocks of a solution that can work
               for both specific and broader use cases. At Benchling, our customers all run their science
               differently, so abstraction and flexibility are critical. We’re looking for clean, extensible design
               as much as working code. You’re welcome to use your own coding environment if that helps
               you work more comfortably.                                           
                                                                                    
            ●  Hiring Manager Behavioral Interview - 45 minutes                     
                                                                                    
               You’ll meet with a member of our Engineering leadership team. They may ask for stories
               from your previous experiences that have shaped you as an engineer.  
                                                                                    
            ●  Recruiter Debrief - 15 minutes                                       
                                                                                    
               You’ll sync back up with your recruiter at the end of the onsite to debrief the interview.
               There’s no need to prepare for this session, though feel free to share any feedback you have
               about the process!                                                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Before the onsite, we'll ask you to have references ready — please try to get them to us as soon as
          possible. We won't reach out to them until later in the process, but having them earlier reduces
          back-and-forth time and lets us run a faster interview process.

          How  to Prepare                                                           
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Interviewing can be quite different from your day-to-day job, so being prepared will help you
          do well. Take some time to brush up on their interview skills, coding skills and algorithms.
                                                                                    
          Practice coding under time pressure. The nature of interviews means you’ll have a limited time for
          coding. Consider practicing coding questions online or doing a mock interview with a friend.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Reflect on your previous experiences. We may ask you to talk through previous projects or stories
          that have impacted you.                                                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Prepare a few questions to ask your interviewers. You’ll have a few minutes in each
          interview to ask questions, so come with a few prepared!                  
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Technical Interview Tips                                                  
                                                                                    
          The interviews will cover topics from coding to algorithms to high-level architectural design. Here
                                                                                    
          are some tips to help prepare:                                            
                                                                                    
          Above all, we want you to do well. We're rooting for you, don't forget that.
                                                                                    
          Use a comfortable coding environment. For the coding interviews, you can choose between
          CoderPad (a shared coding environment) or your coding environment (we'd ask you to share your
          screen). We have starter code for the coding questions you'll need to copy over if you're using your
                                                                                    
          own environment.                                                          
                                                                                    
          Let us know what you're thinking. We care much more about how you solve a problem than about
          whether you solve it or not. We realize that interviewing is noisy, and hearing your thought process
          gives better information than just looking at results. We also know most folks don't usually code and
          talk simultaneously, so don't worry about talking while coding, but be prepared to pause and share
                                                                                    
          occasionally. Be ready to discuss why you made certain decisions, and evaluate the alternatives. We
          don't usually have a single approach in mind for any problem, but you should be able to defend
          whatever path you take.                                                   
                                                                                    
          Feel free to use the internet. We won't ask anything we'd ordinarily need to look up, and if you need
          to look something up during the interview, that's fine. Be comfortable with debugging in your

          Can I use a LLM during the interview?                                     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          We ask that you not use an LLM during the interview. While we recognize that LLMs are a great tool
          and very useful for day-to-day programming, the goal of the interview is to understand how you
                                                                                    
          think and work, not how an LLM thinks and works. We aren't just looking to see if you can solve the
          problems we are giving – we are looking to evaluate your critical thinking and how you approach
          problems. Using an LLM detracts from us being able to understand your problem solving process.
          Instead, it just gives us signal on how you are at prompt engineering which is not what we are looking
          for in these roles.                                                       
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          But you let us use Google and the internet!                               
                                                                                    
          Using Google or the internet typically helps with the implementation of a solution, i.e. "how do I
                                                                                    
          enumerate a list in Python?" or "what is the order of arguments to Javascript's reduce function?" The
          benefit of LLMs over Google is that they work at a much higher level in understanding a more
          abstract problem and then give solutions to that problem. However, even LLMs are limited as the
          problems they can solve are smaller in both scope and constraints than most of the problems than
          you would face during a normal workday. Unfortunately, given the time pressures of an interview
          session, we are asking more limited problems that we can not only find a solution for, but also solve
                                                                                    
          within the time frame. These are problems that an LLM might be able to adequately solve, but these
          aren’t the problems that you’ll be defining and solving at Benchling.     
                                                                                    
          In addition, we are looking to make sure that you understand the problem and your solution, which an
          LLM may not help with. In fact, it may put you at a disadvantage as you may not fully understand the
          code that it recommends.
